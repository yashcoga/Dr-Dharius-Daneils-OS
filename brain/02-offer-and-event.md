### The core problem: "upside down in your relationships"

> "You're making all the deposits, and they're making all the withdrawals."

People keep showing up, giving more, covering gaps and taking responsibility for others until too little is left for their own lives.

**The three patterns:**
- **Overinvested:** giving more time, energy or money than you can sustain.
- **Overcommitted:** being more committed to a relationship, or to someone's progress, than they are.
- **Overextended:** giving so much that you can't be what you need to be for yourself.

Example personas from the brief: the employee doing everyone else's job, the parent continually rescuing an adult child, and the person making all the effort in a relationship.

**Important nuance:** it is *not* "every relationship must be an equal exchange." A child needs care; someone you're helping may not give back. The goal is to stop giving and expecting **without clarity or limits**.

### The mechanism: placement and management

- **Placement:** understand what a relationship actually is, then align **what you expect** and **what you invest** with it. Base decisions on **demonstrated behavior**, not hoped-for potential.
- **Management:** what to do with that clarity, such as changing availability, adjusting expectations, setting a boundary or having an honest conversation. **It does not automatically mean cutting someone off.**
- Key insight: correctly identifying a relationship isn't enough. Even someone who belongs in your life can be part of a draining pattern if the relationship is managed poorly.

**The masterclass's three steps:**
1. **Evaluate:** see what's happening, including your own choices and expectations.
2. **Place:** understand the role the relationship can realistically carry.
3. **Manage:** start changing how you handle it.

### The five seats (not a ranking of anyone's worth)

| Seat | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Friends** | Demonstrated trust, care and mutual support. Closeness alone doesn't establish friendship. |
| **Associates** | Connected through work, activities or shared interests. These connections don't automatically carry friendship's expectations. |
| **Assignments** | People you help, guide or serve. Supporting them doesn't mean they can support you the same way. |
| **Advisors** | People who guide you where they have relevant wisdom or experience. |
| **Family** | ⛔ A new seat added in PD's calls (it isn't in the book). **Its criteria and how overlapping roles are handled are NOT finalized. Name it only; never describe its criteria.** |
