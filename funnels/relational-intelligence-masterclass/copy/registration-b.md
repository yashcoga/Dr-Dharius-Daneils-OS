## 2. The problem

**Eyebrow:** SOUND FAMILIAR?

**Headline:** New Promise. Same Pattern. *Another Disappointment.*

The partner says they'll make more effort. The coworker says you won't have to cover for them again. The friend says they'll be there next time.

So you make room. Give another chance. Expect something different.

And when the same thing happens, you're left wondering whether to explain it again, try harder, or walk away.

People can grow. But there is a difference between leaving room for growth and making today's decisions as if that growth has already happened.

Dr. Daniels teaches that placement starts with what a person's pattern of behavior shows you can realistically expect. **You can hope for change without building every decision around it.**

## 3. What you'll learn

**Eyebrow:** WHAT YOU'LL LEARN

**Headline:** Learn What to Change *When the Pattern Hasn't Changed.*

**Evaluate: Look at the pattern, including your part in it.**
Recognize where your expectations don't match your experience. Examine what you keep giving, assuming, or leaving unspoken, without turning one difficult moment into a verdict on someone.

**Place: Stop asking the relationship to be something it hasn't shown it can be.**
Learn how the five seats, friends, associates, assignments, advisors, and family, clarify roles and expectations. Being close to someone doesn't mean every kind of trust or support belongs there.
*Tags: Friends · Associates · Assignments · Advisors · Family*

**Manage: Choose a next step beyond "try harder."**
Understand when to reconsider an expectation, adjust what you give, or prepare an honest conversation. The goal is a more intentional response, not another reaction you'll regret.

**Supporting copy (closing panel):** You cannot guarantee how someone else responds. You can get clearer about the decisions that belong to you.

## 5. Quick answers

**What if this is someone I can't simply stop seeing?**
The teaching includes relationships within family and work, not just relationships you can easily leave. An adjustment may concern your expectations, availability, or communication rather than ending contact.

**Will this tell me whether to stay or leave?**
No. This is education to help you think and act more intentionally, not a verdict on your relationship.

**Do I need to have read the book?**
No prior reading is required.
