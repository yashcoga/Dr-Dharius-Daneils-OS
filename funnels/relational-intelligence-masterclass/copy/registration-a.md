## 2. The problem

**Eyebrow:** SOUND FAMILIAR?

**Headline:** Being Needed Isn't the Same as *Being Supported.*

You're the person who steps in when a coworker drops the ball. Who keeps making the effort in a relationship. Who answers the call when someone needs help, even when you already have too much on your plate.

But when it's time to work on your own goals, rest, or be present for the people closest to you, there's little left.

Dr. Daniels calls this being **upside down in your relationships**: overcommitted, overinvested, and overextended.

The answer isn't simply to care less. It's to get clearer about the relationship you're in, then align what you expect and what you invest.

## 3. What you'll learn

**Eyebrow:** WHAT YOU'LL LEARN

**Headline:** Learn How to Give With Intention, *Not Until You're Empty.*

**Evaluate: See where you're doing too much.**
Recognize when you're more committed to someone else's progress than they are, or expecting support the relationship hasn't shown it can provide.

**Place: Know what each relationship is meant to carry.**
Explore the five seats: friends, associates, assignments, advisors, and family. Understand why a person you support isn't automatically someone you should depend on for the same support.
*Tags: Friends · Associates · Assignments · Advisors · Family*

**Manage: Know what to do differently.**
Learn how to approach changes in your availability, expectations, and investment, including when an honest conversation or a boundary is needed.

**Supporting copy (closing panel):** Some relationships will involve giving more than you receive. The goal isn't to make every relationship an equal exchange. It's to stop giving without clarity or limits.
