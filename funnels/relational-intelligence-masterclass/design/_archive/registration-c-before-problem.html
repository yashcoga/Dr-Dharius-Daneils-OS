Sound familiar?

Everyone Can Count on You. But You're Running Out of You.

At work, you keep covering the gaps, even when your own responsibilities are piling up.

In a relationship, you're making the plans, starting the conversations, and doing the work to keep things moving.

With family or friends, their needs keep becoming your responsibility. Your needs keep getting postponed.

Dr. Daniels calls this being upside down in your relationships:

  • OverinvestedYou're giving more time, energy, or money than you can sustain.
  • OvercommittedYou're more committed to the relationship, or to someone's progress, than they are.
  • OverextendedWhat you're giving is costing you the capacity to care for yourself and the rest of your life.

You don't necessarily want these people out of your life. You want a way to relate to them that doesn't keep leaving you empty.

A different way forward

Giving More Won't Fix What Needs to Be Realigned.

Sometimes the problem is where you've placed someone: you're expecting a level of support, trust, or care the relationship hasn't shown it can provide.

Other times, the person belongs in your life, but the way you're managing that relationship needs to change.

That's why the answer isn't always "try harder" or "cut them off." It starts with getting clear about what you expect and what you invest.

A relationship doesn't have to be an equal exchange every day. But giving shouldn't mean taking on more than you can carry.
What you'll learn

Three Steps to Start Turning Things Right Side Up

01
Evaluate

See where you're upside down.

Learn to recognize the gap between what a relationship is showing you and what you keep hoping it will become. Look at the pattern, including your own expectations and choices, before deciding what needs to change.

02
Place

Know what each relationship can realistically carry.

Explore the five seats: friends, associates, assignments (people you help or guide), advisors (people who guide you), and family. Understand how these roles help you make wiser decisions about trust, access, and investment.

FriendsAssociatesAssignmentsAdvisorsFamily

The seats describe roles, not a ranking of anyone's worth.

03
Manage

Know what to do with that clarity.

Discover how to approach changes in what you give and expect, when a quiet adjustment may be enough, and when an honest conversation or boundary is needed.

You don't have to wait for everyone else to change before reconsidering how you show up. This masterclass will introduce the approach and help you see where to begin.
